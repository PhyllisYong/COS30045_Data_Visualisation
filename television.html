Televisions & Energy Use

These visualisations were built from the Australian Energy Rating registration data for televisions, to answer a set of research questions about screen technology, screen size, brand and power consumption.

1. What screen technologies are available, and which are most frequent?

LCD (LED) is by far the most common screen technology on the register, well ahead of standard LCD and OLED.

2. What screen sizes are available, and which are most frequent?

65", 75" and 85" screens are registered far more often than any other size, with many smaller and larger sizes appearing only a handful of times.

3. Which brands have the largest number of models?

Kogan, LG and Samsung Electronics have the most registered models, with a steep drop-off after the top four or five brands.

4. Which screen technology consumes the least power?

Standard LCD panels have the lowest median power draw, while LCD (LED) and OLED panels use noticeably more.

5. What is the relationship between screen size and power use?

Power use rises with screen size overall, though the spread grows much wider above about 60 inches.

6. What is the relationship between star rating and screen size?

Star ratings are spread widely across every screen size, so there's no strong or consistent trend between size and efficiency rating.

7. Are there differences in power consumption between brands?

Average power use differs noticeably by brand — Spark Electronics and Emete sit highest — and the box plot shows most brands still have a wide spread of power values across their own model range.

About Us

Appliance Energy Watch is a student project created for COS30045 Data Visualisation, exploring how household appliance data — particularly around televisions — could be presented to help Australian households understand their energy consumption.

Project Goals

  • Practice building a multi-page website with a shared navigation structure.
  • Use JavaScript to control page/content switching without full page reloads.
  • Host the resulting data visualisations, with room to add more from future tasks.
